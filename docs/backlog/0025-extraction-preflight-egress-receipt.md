## User story

As a privacy-cautious user about to upload a PDF of my bloodwork — which carries my name, my date of birth, and my diagnoses — I want a small, honest "what leaves this device" line at the moment I trigger extraction that names exactly where the bytes go (api.anthropic.com, with my own key), what travels (the staged file pages, nothing else), and what stays (everything in IndexedDB, my profile, my prior panels), with an optional one-tap expand that shows the literal egress destination — so that the privacy promise stops being a paragraph in the README I have to take on faith and becomes a verifiable receipt I see at the exact second my most sensitive document is about to move.

## Why now (four lenses)

### Product Owner

The privacy promise is the product's spine, but today it is invisible at the one moment it matters most. When the user stages a lab file and triggers extraction, the file's base64 payload goes to `api.anthropic.com` with no surface that names what is happening — the user is asked to take the most consequential action in the app (transmitting their bloodwork) on the basis of trust they read about elsewhere. The smallest meaningful unit of value is a deterministic, zero-network pre-flight line rendered at the extraction trigger: "<N> page(s) will be sent to api.anthropic.com with your key, for extraction only. Your profile, your prior panels, and these files' originals stay on this device." A single "what travels / what stays" disclosure, expandable to show the literal destination host and the literal list of staged file names, then the existing extraction proceeds unchanged. Nothing about the extraction call changes; the receipt is a render-time truth statement built from the staged-files set and a constant. It makes the contract legible at the point of action, which is where a contract earns trust.

### Stakeholder

This is the **local-first privacy** moat made *visible* — turning a structural property of the architecture into a user-facing artifact. Privacy is the one moat asset we have that a backend-shaped competitor structurally cannot match: they have a server, so something always leaves the device to a party other than the model vendor. We have exactly one egress (`api.anthropic.com`, BYOK, direct) and the README, the AGENTS contract, and the privacy E2E all enforce it. But a moat the user cannot see is a moat that does not convert. The egress receipt makes the single-egress fact concrete at the moment of maximum sensitivity, which is precisely when a privacy-cautious person decides whether to commit. It compounds with the sample tour (0014), which lets a prospect see the artifact before trusting it; the egress receipt lets the prospect verify the privacy claim before trusting it with real data. Two halves of the same trust ladder. It also hardens an existing guarantee into a tested user-facing surface: the receipt's host string is asserted against the same allow-list the privacy spec enforces, so the contract and its display can never drift.

### User (at 7am on the phone)

Sunday, kitchen table, last quarter's labs as a PDF on my phone. I drop the file into the upload area. Before I tap "Extract," a small line appears under the staged file: "1 page will be sent to api.anthropic.com with your key, for extraction only. Everything else stays here." There is a tiny "what travels →" link. I am the kind of person who taps it. It expands: "Travels: the page you staged (labs-q1.pdf). Destination: api.anthropic.com — your key, your call, no server of ours in between. Stays: your profile, your prior panels, the original file." I close it. I tap Extract. It works exactly as before. The receipt did not slow me down — it took the low hum of doubt I always feel when I send medical data somewhere and answered it in one glance. On a flaky connection the receipt still renders instantly, because it is computed locally; only the extraction call itself depends on the network.

### Growth

This is the privacy persona's "show me" screenshot. The Peter-Attia-reading, Cronometer-fatigued, will-not-paste-labs-into-a-chatbot user is exactly who Almanac is for, and the egress receipt is the single image that makes that user's friend say "wait — it tells you exactly what leaves your phone?" No wellness app shows this, because no wellness app *can* — they all have a server, so the honest version of their receipt would read "your data goes to our cloud." (Hypothesis: a screenshot of the egress receipt outperforms the marker hero card (0011) as a top-of-funnel artifact for the privacy-first segment specifically, because it answers the exact objection that segment leads with — "where does my health data go?" — in one frame; back-of-envelope from how Signal's and Proton's "here is what we can't see" screenshots travel in privacy communities — treat as untested). The second-order growth value is that the receipt is also the most honest possible answer to the recurring "isn't this just a Claude wrapper?" critique: the wrapper sends your labs to *its* server; Almanac sends them to *your* Anthropic account, and shows you.

## Acceptance criteria

Each box maps 1:1 to a Playwright test scenario.

- [ ] When at least one file is staged on `#/labs` (the existing `staged: File[]` set in `src/pages/labs.ts`), an egress receipt line renders adjacent to the extract action, BEFORE extraction is triggered. With zero staged files the receipt is absent. Asserted by staging one file and confirming the line appears, then clearing the stage and confirming it disappears.
- [ ] The receipt's summary line reads exactly: "<N> page(s) will be sent to api.anthropic.com with your key, for extraction only. Your profile, your prior panels, and these files stay on this device." where `<N>` is the staged file count and the noun agrees (1 → "page", 2+ → "pages"). Asserted with one staged file (singular) and three staged files (plural).
- [ ] The receipt carries a "what travels →" disclosure (a `role="button"`, ink eyebrow link). Tapping it expands an inline panel listing: (a) "Travels:" followed by the literal staged file names joined with ", "; (b) "Destination: api.anthropic.com"; (c) "Stays: your profile, your prior panels, and the original files." The host string `api.anthropic.com` is rendered verbatim. Asserted by staging two named files and confirming both names render in the "Travels" line and the host renders verbatim.
- [ ] The receipt is computed with **zero network calls** — it reads the staged-files set and a constant host string. Asserted by rendering the receipt (staging files, expanding the disclosure) and confirming the mock recorded zero requests during the receipt render.
- [ ] The host string in the receipt is sourced from the SAME single allow-list constant the privacy layer uses (the extraction endpoint host), not a hardcoded duplicate. Asserted by a test that imports the host constant and confirms the receipt renders that exact value — so the displayed destination can never drift from the enforced egress.
- [ ] Triggering extraction after the receipt renders proceeds through the existing `panelsFromFiles()` path unchanged — the receipt is purely informational and does not gate, delay, or alter the extraction call. Asserted by extracting a fixture file and confirming the resulting panel matches today's behavior byte-for-byte.
- [ ] The receipt also surfaces the extraction-cache fact when it applies: if the staged files' SHA-256 matches a cached extraction (the existing `getCachedExtraction` path), the receipt's summary line additionally reads "These files were already extracted — re-running replays the cached result at no cost and sends nothing." Asserted by staging the same files twice and confirming the second render surfaces the cached-replay line and the extract call fires zero network requests.
- [ ] Manual entry (`#/labs?manual=1`) renders a DIFFERENT, honest receipt: "Manual entry sends nothing — these values are typed straight into this device." No host string, because nothing leaves. Asserted on the manual route.
- [ ] Renders on both **chromium and mobile-webkit**. The disclosure is a ≥44×44 css px touch target; the "Travels" list wraps cleanly at the 390 px viewport when file names are long; the receipt does not push the extract button below the fold on mobile.
- [ ] Shared-view mode (0017) does NOT render the receipt — labs were not part of the shared payload, and the shared-view labs page already renders the "not included" empty state. Asserted in shared-view.
- [ ] Tour mode (0014) renders the receipt against the tour's staged-file fixture but the disclosure makes clear the tour sends nothing: in tour mode the summary reads "In this sample, nothing is sent — extraction is simulated." Asserted in the tour.
- [ ] Privacy E2E (`tests/e2e/privacy.spec.ts`) still passes — the receipt introduces zero new hostnames and asserts the displayed host equals the enforced allow-list host.
- [ ] Schema migration: **no** — the receipt is render-time only and persists nothing.
- [ ] New `tests/e2e/egress-receipt.spec.ts` covers: receipt appears with staged files and disappears when cleared, singular vs plural page count, the disclosure lists literal file names and the verbatim host, zero network during receipt render, host sourced from the allow-list constant, extraction proceeds unchanged after the receipt, cached-replay line surfaces on a repeat stage, manual-entry receipt says nothing is sent, tour-mode and shared-view variants, mobile rendering.

## Out of scope

Explicit anti-goals. The dev agent will not do these even if they seem related.

- A redaction tool that strips name/DOB from the file before sending. Redacting a lab PDF reliably is a hard, error-prone problem; a half-working redactor would create false confidence (the worst privacy failure mode). The honest move is to *show* what travels, not to silently alter it. A redaction feature, if ever, is a separate, carefully-scoped ticket.
- A consent checkbox or a gate that blocks extraction until the receipt is acknowledged. The receipt is informational; adding a blocking step would punish the returning user who already understands the contract. The existing consent gate (welcome) is the one consent surface; this does not add a second.
- Showing the literal base64 payload or a byte count of the transmitted file. The receipt names *what* travels (the pages) and *where* (the host); a raw-bytes view is noise that helps no one and risks rendering a huge string on a phone.
- A network inspector / live request log on the labs page. The Settings → AI calls telemetry surface already accounts for every call; duplicating it here is scope creep. The receipt is a pre-flight statement of intent, not a post-hoc log.
- A per-call "delete this from Anthropic's logs" affordance. We have no server and no control over Anthropic's retention; promising deletion we cannot perform would be dishonest. The receipt states only what is true: the call is direct, BYOK, and ours-no-server.
- Extending the receipt to the plan-compose and meal-generate calls in this ticket. Those calls send structured data (panels, adherence), not raw files, and deserve their own honest framing; this ticket scopes to the lab-upload moment, which is the highest-sensitivity egress (raw medical document). A compose-time receipt is a clean follow-up if this lands well.
- Any change to the egress allow-list or the extraction call itself. This ticket reads the staged set and a constant; it transmits nothing new.

## Engineering notes

Files / patterns the dev should touch. Be specific enough that the dev doesn't have to re-discover the architecture.

- `src/pages/labs.ts` — the upload screen already maintains the module-level `staged: File[]` set and triggers `panelsFromFiles()`. Add a `renderEgressReceipt(staged)` call that paints the summary line + disclosure adjacent to the extract action whenever `staged.length > 0`, re-rendering when the stage mutates (paste/drop/clear). The receipt reads the cache-hit fact via the same SHA-256 hashing the extractor already computes — extract that hash helper if it is currently inline, or call the existing one, so the receipt and the cache decision agree.
- `src/extractor.ts` — if the file-set SHA-256 hashing lives here (it computes the `getCachedExtraction` key), export a small `hashStagedFiles(files: File[]): Promise<string>` so the receipt can ask "is this cached?" without duplicating the hash logic. The cache lookup (`getCachedExtraction`) is already a local-only read.
- `src/share/shared-state.ts` / `src/sample/state.ts` — the receipt's tour and shared-view branches read `isTour()` / `isSharedView()` exactly as the rest of the labs page does; the copy varies per the acceptance bullets.
- The host constant — locate the single source of truth for the Anthropic egress host. The privacy spec enforces an allow-list (`tests/helpers/mocks.ts`); if there is a shared constant in `src/`, import it; if the host currently lives only in the SDK's base URL, introduce one small exported constant (e.g. `ANTHROPIC_HOST = "api.anthropic.com"`) in `src/claude.ts` and have BOTH the receipt and any host display read it, so the displayed destination is provably the enforced one. Do NOT widen anything — this is a read-only constant.
- `src/styles.css` — `.egress-receipt` (the summary line, ink-soft), `.egress-receipt__disclosure` (the "what travels →" eyebrow link), `.egress-receipt__detail` (the expanded panel — "Travels / Destination / Stays" rows). No new colors; ink and ink-soft only. The host string renders in the body weight, not as a link (it is informational, not navigable).
- `tests/e2e/egress-receipt.spec.ts` (new) — every acceptance bullet maps to a `test()`. Re-use `tests/helpers/flows.ts` onboarding + the existing labs staging helpers; stage files via the existing paste/drop test fixtures used by `tests/e2e/labs.spec.ts`.
- `tests/e2e/privacy.spec.ts` — extend with an assertion that the receipt's displayed host equals the enforced allow-list host, and that rendering + expanding the receipt fires zero network requests.
- New deps: **no**.
- Egress allow-list change: **no** — read-only display of the existing single host.
- Schema migration: **no**.
- Voice spec: the summary reads "<N> page(s) will be sent to api.anthropic.com with your key, for extraction only. Your profile, your prior panels, and these files stay on this device." The disclosure label is "what travels →". The detail rows are "Travels:", "Destination:", "Stays:". The manual-entry line is "Manual entry sends nothing — these values are typed straight into this device." Declarative, no exclamation. Banned: "amazing," "exciting," "journey," "great." No oxblood — privacy is calm here, not alarming.
