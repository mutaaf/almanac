## User story

As a user who pays out of pocket to see a functional-medicine practitioner or health coach — and who wants to walk in with my whole record, not a single screenshot — I want a one-tap "Export for my practitioner" that produces a single, printable document packaging my labs summary (every panel, both ranges, flagged against the optimum), the functional-range report card (how many markers sit "normal but not optimal", reusing 0036), my current plan, and my adherence trend, with a plain "generated by Almanac — local-first, BYOK; this person ran their own copy" footer, so that the person I am paying for an hour can read my actual longitudinal picture in two minutes instead of me re-narrating it — and so the tool that prepared it becomes something my practitioner asks their next patient to bring.

## Why now (four lenses)

### Product Owner

The product already has a clinical hand-off, but it is single-plan and single-moment: the printable one-pager (0010, shipped) renders the *current* protocol with the markers behind each recommendation, and the provenance appendix (0016, shipped) adds the rule trail to that one-pager. Neither packages the *longitudinal* record — the thing a functional-medicine practitioner actually reasons from: a chronological labs summary across every draw, the report-card tally that quantifies the "normal but not optimal" gap (0036, shipped), and the adherence record that tells the coach what the patient has actually been holding. The smallest meaningful unit of value is a second, distinct export on the practitioner surface — "Export for my practitioner" — that runs a deterministic, zero-network transform over the same data `exportAll()` already gathers and emits one printable document (the same print-to-PDF mechanism the one-pager already uses) with four sections: a labs summary (every panel, newest first, each result with value, unit, lab range, functional range, flag), the report-card tally (reusing `computeReportCard` from 0036), the current plan (snapshot + insights + eat/avoid/habits/retest, reusing the one-pager's plan render), and an adherence summary (per-habit hit counts over the logged span, reusing the Markdown export's 0027 adherence logic if shipped, else computed inline). It reuses the one-pager's render-and-print path and the report-card and adherence computations; the only new code is the longitudinal labs section, the section assembly, and the practitioner footer. The single-plan one-pager (0010) stays exactly as it is — this is the whole-record sibling, for the appointment where the coach needs the arc, not the snapshot.

### Stakeholder

This turns Almanac from a personal app into **infrastructure a practitioner depends on** — the strongest moat-deepening move available that needs no backend. It compounds three shipped assets at once: the **persistent timeline** (the longitudinal labs section is the timeline made into a clinical document), the **functional-range DB** (every result carries both ranges and the report-card tally quantifies the optimum gap — the exact framing a functional-medicine practitioner already works in), and the **adherence loop** (the coach sees what the patient held, which is the input they would otherwise spend half the session eliciting). It is structurally impossible for a chat box: raw Claude has no prior draws to assemble into a longitudinal summary, no report-card tally, and no adherence record, so "here is my whole picture, formatted for my coach" is a document only a product with a persistent, structured, on-device store can produce. The acquisition channel it opens is the highest-trust one the product has: a practitioner who receives clean Almanac packets from one patient, in the framing they already use, is a practitioner who tells their next patient to bring one — a referral from an authority figure to a captive, motivated audience, earned by the artifact's quality and carrying the wordmark and the local-first/BYOK footer. And it does it without weakening the privacy contract: the document is generated entirely on-device from the patient's own data, printed by the patient, handed over by the patient — Almanac never sees it, because there is nothing to see.

### User (at 7am on the phone)

My functional-medicine appointment is this afternoon and I do not want to spend the first twenty minutes re-explaining my own history. Settings → "Export for my practitioner." One document comes up, ready to print or save as PDF: my draws across the last year, each marker with both ranges and which ones sit normal-but-not-optimal, the tally that says "11 of 38 markers are inside the lab range but below the functional optimum," my current protocol, and what I have actually been holding day to day. I print it, or I AirDrop the PDF to myself and bring it on my phone. My coach reads it in two minutes and we spend the hour on what to change, not on catching them up. At the bottom, a quiet line: "Generated by Almanac — local-first, no account, runs on the patient's own key." My coach asks what it is. That is how the next patient hears about it. It worked offline, instantly, because the whole packet is built from data already on my phone — no call, no wait, no key.

### Growth

This is a moat-deepening ticket with a referral channel no other surface can reach: the practitioner. (Hypothesis: a longitudinal packet formatted in the practitioner's own functional-range framing converts at the highest rate of any acquisition surface the product has, because the recommendation comes from a paid authority to a captive, motivated patient — "bring me one of these next time" — rather than from a peer; and a practitioner who sees clean packets from several patients becomes a standing recommender, a compounding channel that needs no backend, no affiliate program, and no analytics to function; back-of-envelope from how patient-facing tools that fit a clinician's existing workflow get adopted by referral far faster than those a clinician must learn — treat as untested without remote analytics, which we will never add.) The "show me" frame is the document in the practitioner's hands: "my coach asked everyone to start bringing these" is the highest-credibility recommendation the persona can carry back, because it converts the product from a thing they use into a thing their clinician endorses. It is the clinical-channel sibling of the peer-channel artifacts (the improvement card 0047, the share link 0017) — same no-backend, wordmark-carries-the-brand mechanic, aimed at the one audience whose word the patient trusts most.

## Out of scope

Explicit anti-goals. The dev agent will not do these even if they seem related.

- A new PDF/print dependency or a second render stack. The single-plan one-pager (0010) already established the on-device print/PDF mechanism; this packet reuses it verbatim. Do not introduce a new PDF library, html-to-pdf, or a second print path.
- Replacing or restructuring the single-plan one-pager (0010) or its provenance appendix (0016). Those are the snapshot hand-off for a single appointment; this is the longitudinal record. They coexist; this ticket does not touch their templates.
- A second implementation of the report-card tally (0036) or the adherence summary (0027). The packet REUSES `computeReportCard` and the existing adherence computation; if the adherence logic is inlined in the Markdown export, extract the shared function and call it from both — write no parallel count.
- Transmitting the packet anywhere, "send to my coach" email, or a hosted share link. We have no backend. The packet is printed or saved as a file by the patient and handed over by the patient — Almanac never sends it.
- Including the original lab PDF/image blobs. The packet carries the extracted, structured results (the readable clinical record); originals stay on-device as today, exactly as the JSON and Markdown exports already strip blobs.
- A practitioner-side login, portal, or account. There is no practitioner account; the practitioner reads a document the patient brings. No second user model, no auth.
- An LLM-generated clinical summary or narrative for the practitioner. The packet is a deterministic transform of the structured data; a Claude call would add cost, latency, non-determinism, and an egress to a feature whose value is being instant, local, and provably honest. The clinician reads the data, not an AI's interpretation of it.
- A "what to ask your doctor" advice section. We are not a clinician; the standing "informational, not medical advice" disclaimer holds, and the packet presents the patient's own data, not instructions to the practitioner.
- Carrying insight stances (0023) or user-authored rules (0022) into the packet in this ticket. If those ship, a follow-up can add their sections; scoping to the core longitudinal record keeps it shippable now regardless of their status.
